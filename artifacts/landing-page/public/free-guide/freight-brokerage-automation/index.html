The 3 automations, broken down

1

The Spot Quote Engine

$60K net new profit · 20% → 28% win rate

Most brokers quote freight the same way: shipper emails, broker scrambles to check lane history in the TMS, pulls rates from DAT, builds a spreadsheet, fires off a quote. By the time it lands, two other brokers already quoted lower.

We built a system that pulls lane history, live market rates, and our own cost data into one screen. Quote time dropped from 30+ minutes to under 5. Win rate went from 20% to 28%. At $200 average margin per load, that difference alone generated $60K in net new profit in the first year.

The guide covers: how to structure the lane history database, which rate sources to pull from, how to build the quote template so it's accurate and fast, and the exact trigger that fires a quote the moment an email hits your inbox.

2

Carrier Sourcing & Compliance Automation

Zero 3 PM scrambles · Days → hours onboarding

Every broker knows the 3 PM scramble. Load covered — no, wait, carrier's COI expired. Authority pending. Packet incomplete. Meanwhile the shipper's texting "status?" and you're about to lose a customer over a piece of paper.

We automated the entire carrier lifecycle. Sourcing matches carriers to lanes based on historical performance — your best carriers get first look at your best lanes. Onboarding auto-verifies COIs, checks authority status against FMCSA, and tracks document expiration dates. Compliance flags expirations 30 days before they kill a load. What used to take days of back-and-forth now takes hours, and the system catches the paperwork issues before they become load-killing problems.

The guide covers: how to build a carrier scoring model from your own load history, the COI verification workflow that catches expirations automatically, the 7-document packet every carrier needs (and why each one matters), and how to integrate with your TMS so carrier status updates in real time.

3

Client Wallet Share Recovery

$35K+ in hidden freight · 70% of spend is moving elsewhere

Here's the stat that should make every broker uncomfortable: most brokerages capture only 15-30% of their existing clients' total freight spend. The other 70-85% is moving with competitors — not because those competitors are cheaper or better, but because nobody ever asked for the lane.

We built a system that cross-references every shipper's known origin/destination pairs against the lanes we've actually won. It surfaces the gaps automatically — lanes the shipper is clearly moving but we're not touching. One client ran this for 60 days and found $35K in expansion freight hiding in their own book of business. Same shippers. Same relationships. Just lanes nobody thought to ask for.

The guide covers: how to map your shippers' full lane footprint from public data, the lane-gap analysis framework that surfaces expansion opportunities, the "lane request" email template that gets shippers to open up about their other freight, and how to track wallet share growth week over week.
