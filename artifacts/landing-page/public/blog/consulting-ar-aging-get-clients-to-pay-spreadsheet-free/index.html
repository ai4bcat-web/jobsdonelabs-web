The 5-Step Escalation Ladder — Get Paid Without Being the Bad Guy

Most consultants avoid collections because it feels confrontational. The solution is not to get tougher — it is to stop relying on willpower and use a system instead. The escalation ladder turns "chasing payment" into a pre-written sequence. Each step has a script. You do not have to decide what to say — you just execute the next step.

Step 1 — Day 1: Automated "Invoice Attached for Your Records"

Send this the moment the invoice goes out. It is not a follow-up — it is a receipt. It sets the expectation that payment tracking has begun.

"Hi [Contact] — your invoice [INV-###] for [Project Name] is attached. Total: [Amount], due [Date]. Let me know if you need anything else to process this. Thanks!"

Step 2 — Day 15: Friendly "Checking In"

Fifteen days after the due date. Assume good intent. "Maybe it got buried." Re-attach the invoice. Offer help.

"Hi [Contact] — checking in on invoice [INV-###] ([Amount]), which was due on [Date]. Anything you need from my side to get this processed? Re-attached here for convenience. Thanks!"

Step 3 — Day 30: Direct Call Plus Copy to AP

Thirty days past due. Email is not working. Pick up the phone. Call your champion — the person who hired you and values the work. After the call, send a follow-up email copying their AP contact if you have it.

"Hi [Contact] — following up on our call just now. As discussed, invoice [INV-###] for [Amount] (now 30 days past due) needs to be processed this week. I have copied [AP Contact] here. Please let me know if there is a PO number or approval needed that I can help provide. Thanks for moving this forward."

Step 4 — Day 45: Formal Past-Due Notice

Forty-five days past due. The tone shifts from "friendly reminder" to "formal notice." Invoke your late-fee clause. Reference the work-pause provision in your engagement letter.

"Hi [Contact] — Invoice [INV-###] for [Amount] is now 45 days past due. Per our engagement letter dated [Date], late fees of 1.5 percent per month began accruing on day 31 and the outstanding balance is now [Amount + Late Fee]. Per our terms, work on [Project] will pause if the account is not current by [Date — 15 days from now]. I want to avoid that — please let me know today what is needed to process payment. I am available by phone at [Number] if a quick call is easier."

Step 5 — Day 60: Work Pauses Until Account Is Current

Sixty days past due. You have sent four reminders. You have called. The formal notice has expired. The engagement letter provision kicks in. Work stops. This is not an emotional reaction — it is a contract term both parties agreed to.

"Hi [Contact] — as outlined in my notice of [Date], work on [Project] is now paused pending payment of invoice [INV-###] ([Amount] plus accrued late fees). I value our relationship and want to resume work as soon as this is resolved. Please send payment confirmation to [Email] and I will restart immediately. If there is a dispute or concern about the invoice itself, I am ready to discuss — please call me at [Number]."

Every step is pre-written so it is a system, not an emotional reaction. You are not "being aggressive" — you are following the agreed-upon process. The spreadsheet tracks which step each invoice is on so you never have to guess what to send next.

The Late-Fee and Payment-Term Framework Your Engagement Letter Is Missing

Standard "net 30" is toothless without consequences. Most consulting engagement letters specify when payment is due but are silent on what happens when it is late. That silence is expensive.

Add these three clauses to your next engagement letter:

Three clauses that change payment behavior

1. Late fee: "Invoices not paid within 30 days of the invoice date are subject to a late fee of 1.5 percent per month (18 percent annual) on the outstanding balance. Late fees begin accruing on day 31."

2. Right to pause work: "The Consultant reserves the right to pause all work on this engagement if any invoice remains unpaid 45 days past its due date. Work will resume within one business day of payment confirmation."

3. Retainer conversion option: "For clients with two or more late payments in a 12-month period, the Consultant may require conversion to a retainer billing model with automatic monthly payment for all future engagements."

The 1.5 percent monthly late fee (18 percent annual) is legal in all 50 US states when properly disclosed in a signed agreement. It compensates you for the cost of capital and creates a financial incentive for on-time payment that "net 30" alone does not.

How to Introduce These Terms to Existing Clients

You do not need to have a confrontational "new policy" meeting. Send a brief email before the next engagement:

"Hi [Client] — we are standardizing our billing practices across all engagements. Starting with our next project, our payment terms include a 1.5 percent monthly late fee on balances past 30 days. This aligns our billing with industry standards and ensures our team can focus on delivering great work instead of chasing payments. The new terms are reflected in the engagement letter attached. No action needed — just flagging so you are aware. Appreciate you!"

Most clients accept this without pushback. Those who push back are often the ones who planned to pay late — and the conversation itself is valuable data for your client risk score.

The Client-by-Client Payment Risk Score

Not all clients pay the same way. The spreadsheet automatically calculates a risk score for each client based on their payment history:

  • Green — pays within 30 days, no broken promises. These clients get standard net-30 terms. No special handling needed. They are your best accounts — protect them.
  • Yellow — average 31 to 60 days DSO or one broken payment promise. These clients get automated reminders starting at day 7 instead of day 15. Have a conversation about retainer conversion at the next renewal. A yellow client who converts to retainer becomes a green client overnight.
  • Red — average 60 plus days DSO or two or more broken promises. These clients require prepayment or retainer on the next engagement. If they refuse, you have a decision: accept that you are effectively their bank (and price accordingly — add 15 to 20 percent to compensate for the float), or fire them and replace with a client who pays on time.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get consulting clients to pay on time?

The root cause of late payments is almost never the client's AP department — it is the absence of a systematic follow-up process on your side. Fix it with three things: (1) an AR aging report that shows every outstanding invoice by days overdue so nothing falls through the cracks, (2) a pre-written escalation ladder (friendly reminder at day 15, direct call at day 30, formal past-due notice at day 45, work-pause provision at day 60) so follow-up is automatic instead of emotional, and (3) payment terms with teeth — a 1.5 percent monthly late fee and a right-to-pause-work clause in your engagement letter. Most consulting firms that implement all three see DSO drop from 45-60 days to 25-35 days within two billing cycles.

What should an AR aging report for a consulting firm include?

Client name, project, invoice number, invoice date, due date, amount, days outstanding, aging bucket (0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+), follow-up count, last contact date, promised payment date, escalation level, and notes. The dashboard should show total outstanding AR, weighted-average DSO, top 10 overdue balances, aging distribution chart, DSO trend over time, and client risk scores. Our free spreadsheet includes all of these.

How do I handle a client who is 90 days past due?

Send a final demand letter via certified mail with a 7-business-day deadline citing your late-fee clause. If the engagement is ongoing, invoke your right-to-pause-work provision and stop all active work. Offer a payment plan only if the client has genuine cash-flow issues and you want to preserve the relationship. If no response after the certified letter, send the account to collections or small claims court. Document every contact, promise, and broken promise — courts and collection agencies need the paper trail.

Should I charge late fees to consulting clients?

Yes — but only if disclosed upfront in your engagement letter. A 1.5 percent monthly late fee (18 percent annual) is legal in all 50 US states when properly disclosed and is the industry standard for professional services. The late fee compensates you for the cost of floating the receivable and creates a financial incentive for on-time payment. Most clients accept it without pushback when introduced as a standard billing practice rather than a punishment.

What is a normal DSO for a professional services firm?

The median DSO for professional services firms is 35 to 45 days. Top-quartile firms operate at 25 to 30 days. Bottom-quartile firms run 55 to 75 days. If your DSO is above 45 days, you are in the bottom half. A 10-day DSO improvement on 500 thousand dollars in annual revenue frees up roughly 14 thousand dollars in cash. The fastest fix: the AR aging spreadsheet plus escalation ladder plus a late-fee clause in your engagement letter.
