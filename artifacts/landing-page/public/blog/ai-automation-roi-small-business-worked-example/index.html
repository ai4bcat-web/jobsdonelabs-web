What's the Real ROI of AI Automation for a Small Business? A Worked Example You Can Steal

By Ryne Bandolik · Jobs Done Labs · August 29, 2026

Key takeaways

A 10-person service business saves $9,100/year on a $2,000 automation — it pays for itself in 11 weeks. The 3-year ROI is 1,265%. The highest returns come from automating data entry, lead follow-up, invoicing, and client document chasing. The ROI formula is simple: (Annual Savings – Automation Cost) / Automation Cost × 100. This post includes a complete worked example, a business-size comparison table, and a process-by-process ROI table you can apply to your own company today.

The Math: A Worked Example for a 10-Person Business

Most small business owners know automation saves time. But nobody shows you the actual math. So here it is — a real worked example for a 10-person home services or professional services company.

The business has an office manager who spends 7 hours every week on three repetitive tasks:

  • Data entry (3 hrs/wk): copying new leads from email into the CRM, updating QuickBooks with job details that are already in the field app, reconciling spreadsheets that don't talk to each other.
  • Lead follow-up (2 hrs/wk): manually texting or emailing every new lead, remembering who hasn't responded, tracking which leads are hot vs. cold.
  • Invoicing (2 hrs/wk): exporting time sheets, building invoices in Word, emailing them one at a time, following up on unpaid ones.

At a fully-loaded hourly cost of $25/hour (wages, payroll taxes, benefits, software), those 7 hours cost $175/week. Over a 50-week working year, that's $8,750/year — just for one person's admin work.

The automation: A custom workflow that connects the CRM, QuickBooks, and email — lead data auto-populates, invoices generate from job records, and follow-up sequences fire automatically. Build cost: $2,000 (one-time, built by an automation agency like Jobs Done Labs). No ongoing monthly fees beyond the tools you already pay for.

The ROI Formula (Steal This)

Here's the formula every business owner should keep in their back pocket:

ROI = (Annual Savings – Automation Cost) / Automation Cost × 100

How to use it in 4 steps:

  1. Pick one process you want to automate. The best candidate is something you or your team does every single day that involves copying data from one place to another.
  2. Time it. Track exactly how many hours per week that process consumes. Use a stopwatch for one week — don't estimate. Most owners underestimate by 40-60%.
  3. Annualize the cost. Hours/week × fully-loaded hourly rate × 50 weeks = Annual Labor Cost. Use your actual payroll cost, not just salary — include taxes, benefits, and the software you pay for while that person does data entry instead of higher-value work.
  4. Plug in the build cost. Get a real quote for the automation. Free templates cost $0 (but your time to set them up). No-code tools cost $20-200/month. Custom builds from an agency typically run $1,500-8,000 for a single-process automation.

Real example using the formula:
Process: manual data entry between CRM and QuickBooks
Hours/week: 4 hours
Hourly rate: $25
Annual labor cost: 4 × $25 × 50 = $5,000/year
Automation build cost: $1,500

Year 1 ROI = ($5,000 – $1,500) / $1,500 × 100 = 233%
3-Year ROI = ($15,000 – $1,500) / $1,500 × 100 = 900%
Payback period: $1,500 / ($5,000 ÷ 50) = 15 weeks

Why "Average ROI" Is a Trap — and What Actually Matters

You'll see automation vendors cite numbers like "400% average ROI" or "30% cost reduction." Those are marketing numbers, not your numbers. Here's what actually drives ROI in the real world:

  • Process selection matters more than technology. Automating a process that costs you $500/year in labor won't move the needle, no matter how clever the automation is. Automating a $15,000/year data-entry bottleneck? That changes your P&L. Pick the biggest, dumbest, most repetitive task first.
  • Adoption is the silent ROI killer. The best automation in the world delivers $0 if your team doesn't use it. The automations with the highest ROI are the ones that work invisibly — no training, no new logins, no behavior change. The data just moves.
  • Hidden savings are real. The ROI formula above counts direct labor savings. It doesn't count: errors that stop happening (no more $500 billing mistakes), leads that stop falling through cracks (3-4 extra jobs/month), or the manager who now spends those saved hours on growth instead of admin.
  • Compounding is everything. A $2,000 automation that saves $8,750/year isn't a one-year win. It's a $8,750/year win forever. Over 5 years, that's $43,750 in savings for $2,000. Most capital investments — new equipment, new hires, new software — don't come close to that return profile.

Frequently asked questions

What's the typical ROI of business automation for a small company?

For a 10-person small business, automation typically delivers 300-1,200% ROI over 3 years. A $2,000 custom automation that saves 7 hours/week pays for itself in 11 weeks and returns $27,300 in labor savings over 3 years — a 1,265% ROI. The exact ROI depends on which processes you automate (data entry, lead follow-up, and invoicing deliver the fastest returns), your labor costs, and whether you use free templates, no-code tools, or a custom-build agency.

How do you calculate the ROI of automating a business process?

The formula is: ROI = (Total Savings – Automation Cost) / Automation Cost × 100. To calculate: identify the task, measure hours/week it consumes, multiply by your fully-loaded hourly rate, annualize (×50 weeks), subtract the automation build cost, then divide by the build cost and multiply by 100. A worked example: 7 hrs/week × $25/hr × 50 weeks = $8,750/year. If the automation costs $2,000, Year 1 ROI = ($8,750 – $2,000) / $2,000 × 100 = 338%. Three-year ROI increases to 1,265% because the savings repeat every year while the build cost stays fixed.

Which business processes have the best ROI when automated?

The highest-ROI automations target repetitive, high-volume admin work. The top five: (1) invoice generation and follow-up (5-10 hrs/week saved), (2) lead response and follow-up (captures 30-50% more leads), (3) manual data entry between systems (eliminates 3-15 hrs/week of copy-paste errors), (4) client document chasing (cuts 4-8 hrs/week of email follow-up), (5) monthly reporting and dashboard updates (saves 4-12 hrs/week). These five processes alone deliver 300-1,200% ROI for a typical 10-person business.

How long does it take for automation to pay for itself?

Most small-business automations pay for themselves in 6-16 weeks. A $2,000 custom build saving 7 hours/week at $25/hour breaks even in 11 weeks. Free templates and no-code tools ($20-200/month) pay back in days or weeks — the cost is low enough that even one hour saved per week justifies the expense. Larger custom builds ($10,000-30,000) typically pay back in 4-8 months. The fastest payback comes from targeting your highest-hour, most-repetitive tasks first.
