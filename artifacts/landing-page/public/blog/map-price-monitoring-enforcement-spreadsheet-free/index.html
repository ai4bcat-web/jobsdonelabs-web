Section 1: MAP product registry — know what you are protecting

Before you can track violations, you need a single source of truth for what MAP actually is on every product. The first tab of the spreadsheet is your product registry.

What goes in the product registry:

  • SKU — your internal product identifier
  • Product name — the consumer-facing name
  • ASIN / UPC / EAN — marketplace identifiers (one column per marketplace)
  • MAP price — the minimum advertised price you set
  • Wholesale cost — what authorized retailers pay you
  • MSRP — suggested retail (for reference only — MAP is what you enforce)
  • MAP policy date — when you last updated the policy for this SKU
  • Penalty policy — first offense (warning letter), second offense (suspension), third offense (termination)

This tab is your brand protection command center. Every violation you log will reference a SKU from this registry. When an authorized retailer says "I did not know the MAP changed," you point to this sheet.

Section 2: Violation log — capture every rogue seller

This is the heart of the system. Every time you spot a seller advertising below your MAP, you log it here. Over time, this log becomes your evidence file.

What the violation log captures:

  • Date detected — when you first saw the violation
  • Seller name — the storefront name (e.g., DiscountKing99)
  • Marketplace — Amazon, Walmart, eBay, Google Shopping, or other
  • Listing URL — direct link to the violating listing
  • SKU affected — reference to your product registry
  • Advertised price — the price they are showing
  • Dollars below MAP — calculated automatically (MAP minus advertised price)
  • Percentage below MAP — how far under they are (e.g., 20 percent below)
  • Holding Buy Box? — yes or no (check manually when you log)
  • Screenshot link — a link to the screenshot you saved (Google Drive, Dropbox, or local file path)
  • Status — open, enforcement sent, resolved, or escalated
Pro tip: Screenshot everything

Amazon Brand Registry and most marketplace enforcement teams require a screenshot or a link to the live listing as evidence. Before you send a single enforcement email, take a full-page screenshot showing the seller name, the advertised price, and the listing URL. Save it in a dated folder. Link it in the violation log. This one habit is the difference between a case that gets resolved in 48 hours and one that gets dismissed for "insufficient evidence."

Section 3: Repeat-offender dashboard — who is costing you the most?

Most rogue sellers are not one-time offenders. They test your enforcement response and, if you do nothing, they keep going. The repeat-offender dashboard aggregates your violation log and shows you exactly who to prioritize.

The dashboard auto-calculates for each seller:

  • Number of violations — total violations logged
  • Worst violation — the deepest price cut (e.g., 12 dollars below MAP)
  • Last violation date — are they still active?
  • Estimated revenue impact — sum of lost Buy Box revenue across all violations (calculated from Section 4)
  • Enforcement status — have you sent a warning? Filed a case? What was the outcome?
  • Priority score — auto-ranked by total revenue impact (highest at the top)

Sort by priority score every Monday morning. The seller at the top is costing your brand the most money. Start there.

Section 4: Revenue-impact calculator — what each violation is actually costing you

This is the number that motivates action. Most brand owners underestimate how much a MAP violation costs because they think only about the lost sale on the violating listing. The real cost is much larger.

The revenue-impact calculator uses three inputs:

  • Daily sales at MAP price — how many units per day this SKU sells through authorized channels
  • Buy Box loss percentage — what share of Buy Box ownership you lose when a rogue seller undercuts (typically 40 to 60 percent)
  • Days at violation price — how long the violation has been active

Formula: Daily Sales × Buy Box Loss Percentage × Days at Violation Price × MAP Price = Estimated Lost Revenue

Worked example: Your flagship SKU sells 5 units per day at forty nine dollars and ninety nine cents MAP. A rogue seller holds the Buy Box at thirty nine dollars and ninety nine cents for fourteen days. You lose 50 percent of Buy Box ownership.

5 units × 50 percent loss × 14 days × forty nine dollars and ninety nine cents = approximately one thousand seven hundred fifty dollars in lost revenue from a single violation.

Add the wholesale account risk — the customer who calls and says "I am dropping your line" — and the real cost of unenforced MAP becomes existential.

Section 5: Enforcement action tracker — from warning letter to Brand Registry case

The fourth tab is your enforcement command center. Logging violations is step one. Acting on them — and tracking what happens — is what actually protects your brand.

What the enforcement tracker captures:

  • Violation ID — reference to the violation log entry
  • Seller contacted — the rogue seller's storefront name
  • Action taken — warning email sent, policy reminder, cease-and-desist, marketplace complaint filed
  • Date of action — when you sent it
  • Method — email, Amazon Brand Registry complaint, marketplace seller support ticket, legal letter
  • Response received? — yes, no, or partial (did the seller respond?)
  • Seller response — what they said (e.g., "price was a mistake — corrected")
  • Price corrected? — did the advertised price return to MAP or above?
  • Date resolved — when the violation was fully resolved
  • Amazon Brand Registry case number — if a formal complaint was filed
  • Outcome — resolved, escalated to legal, seller banned, or unresolved

Enforcement escalation ladder

Follow this sequence for every violation. Do not skip steps — marketplace enforcement teams expect documentation of good-faith attempts to resolve directly before they will intervene.

  1. First offense: Send a polite but firm email referencing your MAP policy and the specific violation. Include a screenshot of the listing. Ask them to adjust the price within 48 hours.
  2. Second offense (same seller): Send a formal cease-and-desist email. Reference the first violation and warn that continued non-compliance will result in suspension of their authorized reseller status and a complaint to the marketplace.
  3. Third offense (same seller): File an Amazon Brand Registry complaint citing trademark or copyright infringement where applicable. Attach the violation log showing the pattern of repeated violations. Simultaneously send the seller a termination-of-reseller-status letter.
  4. Ongoing (any seller): If a seller is not an authorized reseller and refuses to comply, escalate directly to the marketplace's intellectual property or brand protection team with your full case file.

Section 6: Unauthorized seller list — know who should not be selling your product

The final tab is a running list of every unauthorized seller you have encountered. This list serves two purposes: it prevents you from accidentally onboarding a known rogue seller as an authorized account, and it gives your sales team a blocklist for wholesale applications.

Each unauthorized seller entry includes:

  • Seller name — storefront or business name
  • Marketplace(s) — where they are active
  • First detected — when you first noticed them selling your product
  • Products affected — which of your SKUs they are listing
  • Contact attempted? — have you tried to reach them?
  • Status — active (still selling), blocked (marketplace removed them), converted (became authorized), or unknown
  • Notes — any relevant history or patterns

How the six tabs work together

Here is the Monday morning workflow — twenty minutes, once per week, and your MAP enforcement is no longer a Gmail folder:

  1. Product registry (Tab 1): Verify MAP prices are current. Update any policy changes.
  2. Violation log (Tab 2): Check your marketplaces for new violations. Log any you find — including screenshots.
  3. Repeat-offender dashboard (Tab 3): Sort by priority score. Who is costing you the most? That seller is your number one enforcement target this week.
  4. Revenue-impact calculator (Tab 4): For the top three offenders, run the numbers. Quantify what each is costing you. This is the data you take to your wholesale accounts when they ask why MAP is not being enforced.
  5. Enforcement action tracker (Tab 5): Send follow-ups on any open enforcement actions. Escalate to Brand Registry for any seller on their third violation. Log outcomes.
  6. Unauthorized seller list (Tab 6): Review for any new unauthorized sellers your team has spotted. Cross-check against new wholesale account applications.

Frequently asked questions

What is MAP and how is it different from MSRP?

MAP (Minimum Advertised Price) is the lowest price a retailer is allowed to advertise your product — it applies to the displayed price on websites, marketplaces, and advertisements. MSRP (Manufacturer's Suggested Retail Price) is exactly what it sounds like — a suggestion with no enforcement mechanism. MAP is what you enforce through a unilateral pricing policy and marketplace complaints. This spreadsheet tracks MAP violations, not MSRP deviations.

Do I need to be in Amazon Brand Registry to enforce MAP?

You do not need Brand Registry to send warning emails to rogue sellers or to track violations. But Brand Registry gives you access to Amazon's formal complaint process for intellectual property and listing violations, which carries more weight than a seller-to-seller message. If you own a registered trademark, enroll in Brand Registry — it is free and significantly strengthens your enforcement position.

How often should I check for MAP violations?

For a brand with 1 to 15 SKUs: check weekly. For 15 to 30 SKUs: check twice per week, or spot-check your top 10 SKUs daily. The damage from a MAP violation compounds over time — a violation caught in 3 days costs far less than one caught in 3 weeks. The twenty-minute Monday morning review described above is the minimum sustainable cadence.

What if the rogue seller is not an authorized reseller?

This is very common — unauthorized sellers obtain your product through distribution leaks, retail arbitrage, or liquidation channels. You still log them in the violation log and the unauthorized seller list. Send them a polite message asking them to stop selling your product or comply with MAP. If they refuse, escalate to the marketplace. Amazon Brand Registry complaints against unauthorized sellers for trademark infringement are often effective if the seller is using your brand name in the listing without permission.

Can I use this spreadsheet for multiple marketplaces?

Yes. The violation log has a Marketplace column (Amazon, Walmart, eBay, Google Shopping, or custom). The product registry has separate columns for marketplace-specific identifiers (ASIN for Amazon, UPC for Walmart, etc.). The dashboard and revenue calculator are marketplace-agnostic — they work across all channels. One spreadsheet covers your entire brand.

When should I upgrade from this free spreadsheet to a paid MAP monitoring tool?

Upgrade when: (a) you exceed 30 SKUs and manual weekly checks become unsustainable, (b) you are losing more than three thousand dollars per month to MAP violations and the SaaS fee is less than the loss, or (c) you sell across more than 5 marketplaces and cannot manually check them all. Until any of those thresholds are met, the spreadsheet is more than sufficient — and it keeps six thousand to twelve thousand dollars per year in your pocket.
