You Bought a Client Portal So Clients Would Stop Emailing Sensitive Documents — But They're Still Sending Everything Over Unencrypted Email and It's Doubling Your Admin Work (Free Portal Adoption Playbook)

By Ryne Bandolik · Jobs Done Labs · July 27, 2026

The portal is there. It works. You sent the welcome email. And yet your inbox this morning had 14 client emails with sensitive documents attached, and your office manager spent 90 minutes downloading them and re-uploading them to the portal.

That's not a technology problem. It's a behavioral-change problem — and nobody sells you the behavioral-change system when they sell you the portal. The vendor's answer is always "send them another reminder email." You've sent five. They're still emailing you. Here's why, and here's the actual fix.

Why clients default to email (it's not because they're difficult)

Before you can fix the behavior, you have to understand why it happens. Clients don't email documents to spite you. They email because:

  1. Email is already open. The document is on their desktop. Gmail or Outlook is already running. Attaching a file takes 3 seconds. Logging into a portal they visit twice a year takes 30 seconds plus password recovery — and they don't remember the password.
  2. Nobody set the expectation at onboarding. The portal welcome email — if you sent one — arrived during onboarding week when the client was already drowning in 17 other emails from you. They skimmed it, didn't log in, and three months later they have a tax document to send and they open Gmail.
  3. Nobody explained what's in it for THEM. Every portal pitch is about what it does for the FIRM — "secure, compliant, organized." The client hears: "I bought software to manage YOU." What the client needs to hear: "This protects YOUR data from the 14 people who could intercept an unencrypted email, and it means YOUR documents never get lost in someone's inbox."
  4. The mobile experience is terrible. Most client portals are designed for desktop lawyers and accountants. When a client tries to upload a photo of a W-2 from their phone, the experience is so bad they give up and email it instead.

Part 1: The 3-email onboarding drip that sets the expectation

This sequence goes out to every new client automatically. If you have existing clients who've never adopted the portal, send them the same sequence as a "portal re-launch" — frame it as a security upgrade, not a "you've been doing it wrong" message.

Email 1 — Day 0 (immediately after engagement letter signed): "Your secure client portal is ready"

Subject: Your secure portal is ready — here's how to access it (30 seconds)

Hi [Name],

Your secure client portal is live. Going forward, this is where we'll exchange all documents — tax returns, bank statements, contracts, everything. Here's why:

Email isn't encrypted. When you attach a tax return or bank statement to an email, that document passes through multiple servers before it reaches us — and any of them could be intercepted. The portal encrypts everything end-to-end, which means your financial data stays between you and us. Period.

Nothing gets lost. Every document you upload goes straight into your file — not buried in someone's inbox under 200 other emails. When we need something from you, the request shows up in your portal dashboard, and when you upload it, we're notified immediately.

Here's how to log in (takes 30 seconds):
→ Go to [portal URL]
→ Your username: [email]
→ Temporary password: [password] (you'll set your own on first login)
→ Bookmark this page or save it to your phone's home screen — you'll use it every time we need documents.

If you're on your phone, [portal name] has a mobile app — download it here: [app store link]. It takes 30 seconds to install and you can upload documents by taking a photo.

Reply to this email if you have any trouble logging in — I'll walk you through it.

Email 2 — Day 3: "Here's what happens when we need something from you"

Subject: How document requests work (so nothing falls through the cracks)

Hi [Name],

Quick follow-up: when we need a document from you — say, last year's tax return or a bank statement — here's exactly what happens:

  1. You'll get a notification from the portal (email or push, whichever you prefer) saying "New document request from [Firm Name]."
  2. Click the link, log in (face ID or fingerprint on mobile), and you'll see exactly what we need and a button to upload it.
  3. Upload the document — drag and drop on desktop, or take a photo on mobile. Done.
  4. We get notified instantly that it's there. No "did you get my email?" follow-ups needed.

Important: If you email us a document instead of using the portal, you'll get an automatic reply asking you to re-upload it through the portal. This isn't us being difficult — it's because emailed documents aren't encrypted and we have a firm policy to protect your data. More on that in email #3.

Bookmark your portal: [portal URL]

Email 3 — Day 7: "Our secure document policy (and why it protects YOU)"

Subject: Our document security policy — what it means for you

Hi [Name],

Last email in this series — and the most important one. Here's our document handling policy in plain English:

All client documents go through the portal. No exceptions. This isn't about convenience — it's about protecting your data. Email is the #1 vector for financial data breaches at professional service firms. A single intercepted email with your tax return attached could expose your Social Security number, bank account numbers, income history, and dependents' information. The portal encrypts everything so that even if someone intercepted the transmission, they couldn't read it.

What if you email us a document by accident? You'll get an auto-reply within 60 seconds: "I received your email. For your security, please upload this document through your portal here: [link]." It's not a scolding — it's a guardrail. And it keeps your data safe.

What if you really can't use the portal? Call us. We'll figure it out. But for 99% of document exchanges, the portal is faster, safer, and creates a permanent record of exactly what was sent and when — which protects YOU if there's ever a question about what was submitted.

That's it. Three emails, seven days, and you're set up for secure document exchange from here on. If you ever have trouble with the portal, reply to this email or call [phone]. We'll get you sorted in under 5 minutes.

Part 2: The client-facing one-pager (brand it, send it, keep it handy)

Some clients need a physical (or PDF) reference. Create a one-page document — your firm's logo at the top — that answers three questions in under 200 words total:

"Why we use a portal" — Client one-pager template

  1. Why a portal? "Email isn't encrypted — anyone between you and us can read an attachment. The portal encrypts your data so only you and our team can see it. This is the same security standard banks use."
  2. How to access: URL, your username (your email), how to reset your password (link), and a QR code that opens the portal on a phone camera.
  3. When to use it: "Any time you're sending us a document — tax returns, bank statements, contracts, IDs, anything with personal or financial information. If you're not sure, use the portal."
  4. What happens if you email instead: "You'll get an automatic reply asking you to upload through the portal. This isn't personal — it's our firm's security policy, applied equally to every client. Your data is too important to sit in an unencrypted inbox."
  5. Need help? Phone number, email, and "We'll walk you through it in under 5 minutes. Seriously."

Save this as a PDF. Attach it to Email #1 in the onboarding drip. Keep a printed copy in your office for the clients who visit in person. It's the one reference document that answers every portal question before it becomes a support call.

Part 3: The email-to-portal auto-reply (the nudge that changes behavior)

This is the single highest-leverage component. When a client emails a document, they get an immediate, warm auto-reply that redirects them to the portal. Set it up as a rule in your email client (Gmail filters, Outlook rules, or your email provider's auto-responder):

Auto-reply template (copy-paste ready):

Subject: Got your email — one quick step to keep your data secure

Hi [Name],

I saw your email come through — thanks for sending this over.

Quick request: To keep your financial data encrypted and secure, we process all documents through your client portal. Could you upload this file there instead? Here's your portal link: [URL]

Sending documents over regular email is like mailing a bank statement in a clear envelope — anyone between you and us can potentially see it. The portal encrypts everything so only you and our team can access it.

It takes 30 seconds: click the link, log in (Face ID or fingerprint on your phone), drag and drop the file, done. If you've already uploaded it there by the time you read this — thank you, and ignore this message.

If you need help accessing the portal, reply to this email or call [phone] — we'll walk you through it.

Thanks,
[Your name]

Setup instructions:

  • Gmail: Settings → Filters and Blocked Addresses → Create a new filter. For "Has the words," enter: "has:attachment". Then choose "Send template" and paste the auto-reply above. Pro tip: exclude emails from your team domain so internal attachments don't trigger it.
  • Outlook: Rules → New Rule → Apply rule on messages I receive → with specific words in the body (use "attach" as a trigger word) → have server reply using [template].
  • Microsoft 365/Exchange: Mail flow rules → Apply rule if attachment size > 0 bytes and sender is external → reply with custom message.

The magic of the auto-reply: it works 24/7 without anyone on your team lifting a finger. It's polite, educational (explains WHY), and redirects to the portal with a single click. Most clients comply on the first nudge because nobody ever told them email wasn't secure — they just didn't know.

Part 4: The portal-adoption tracking spreadsheet (measure it or it won't stick)

You can't improve what you don't measure. A simple Google Sheet tracks whether the system is actually working:

Portal Adoption Tracker — 6 columns, 5 minutes/week

  1. Week. Week ending date.
  2. Documents received — portal. Count of documents that arrived via portal upload.
  3. Documents received — email. Count of documents that arrived via email attachment.
  4. Portal adoption rate. Portal / (Portal + Email) × 100. Target: 80%+ by day 60.
  5. Auto-replies sent. How many times the email-to-portal nudge fired this week.
  6. Admin hours saved. Estimate: (portal docs this week × 2.5 minutes) — the time NOT spent downloading from email and uploading to portal.

One person owns this spreadsheet. Five minutes every Friday. After 8 weeks, you'll have a trend line that tells you whether the system is working — and a hard number for how many admin hours you've recovered. For a firm with 200 active clients, expect 4-6 hours/week recovered once adoption hits 80%.

Frequently asked questions

Why do clients keep emailing documents instead of using the portal?

Clients default to email because it's already open, it's the habit they've built over years, and nobody set the portal expectation at onboarding. Most firms buy a portal, send a single "here's your login" email, and hope clients switch — but behavioral change doesn't work that way. Clients need three things to switch: (1) the expectation set at the very first interaction, (2) a clear reason why the portal protects THEM (not just the firm), and (3) frictionless access — if the login process takes more than 30 seconds or the mobile experience is terrible, they'll default back to email. The free playbook above gives you the 3-email onboarding drip, client-facing one-pager, and automated nudge system that actually changes behavior.

How do I enforce a portal-only policy without losing clients?

Enforcement doesn't mean "block email" — it means making the portal the path of least resistance while gracefully redirecting email. Three tactics work: (1) Set the expectation at onboarding with a 3-email drip that explains why the portal protects the client's data, how to log in, and what happens if they email documents instead (they get a polite auto-reply redirecting to the portal). (2) Implement an automated email-to-portal nudge: when a client emails a document, they get an immediate reply — "I received your email. For security, please upload this through your portal here: [link]. Documents sent via email aren't encrypted and put your data at risk." (3) Never say "you can't email us" — say "we process everything through the portal so nothing falls through the cracks." Clients accept systems that work for them.
