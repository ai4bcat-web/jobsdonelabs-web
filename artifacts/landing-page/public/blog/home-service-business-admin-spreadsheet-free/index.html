The Four Disconnected Tools — and the One Spreadsheet That Replaces All of Them

Every trade runs the same stack: a whiteboard for scheduling, QuickBooks for invoicing, a notebook for estimate follow-ups, and the owner's memory for who still owes money. The tools do not talk to each other. When a job is completed on the whiteboard, nobody tells QuickBooks to generate the invoice. When an invoice goes unpaid, nobody tells the notebook to flag that customer for follow-up. When an estimate sits unresponded for three days, nobody reminds the owner to make the call.

This spreadsheet connects all four workflows. Here is what is inside:

1. Customer Master — The CRM You Never Had

Before any job is scheduled or any invoice is sent, you need a single place where every customer lives. This tab is your customer roster with:

  • Customer name, address, phone, email
  • Service history — every job, date, technician, and what was done
  • Equipment installed — furnace model, AC unit, water heater, with serial numbers and install dates
  • Last service date — so you know who is due for maintenance
  • Notes and preferences — gate code, dog on premises, customer prefers afternoon appointments, do not ring doorbell
  • Total lifetime value — auto-calculated so you know who your best customers are

When a customer calls, you type their name and see everything in one row. No more flipping through thirty pages of a spiral notebook looking for the last service date.

2. Job Scheduler — Replaces the Whiteboard

A weekly view, one row per technician, showing every job in time order:

  • Technician name (column headers)
  • Customer name, address, job type (repair, install, maintenance, estimate)
  • Estimated duration (auto-populated: a furnace repair is 2 hours, an AC install is 6 hours, an estimate is 1 hour)
  • Drive time between jobs (auto-calculated from zip codes)
  • Status: Scheduled, In Progress, Done, Invoiced, Paid
  • Emergency slots reserved — one per day per technician, so a 3 PM no-heat call does not blow up the entire schedule

The scheduler auto-flags when a technician is double-booked or when drive time plus job duration means they will not make the next appointment. No more refunds for missed windows.

3. Estimate Tracker — The Revenue Recovery Engine

This is where the money lives. Every estimate you give goes into this tab:

  • Date given, customer, job description, estimate amount
  • Follow-up calls: date, notes (called — left voicemail; called — customer wants to think about it; called — customer ready to book)
  • Status: Pending, Followed Up (Day 1), Followed Up (Day 3), Followed Up (Day 7), Won, Lost
  • Auto-flag: Any estimate not followed up within 3 days turns red. You see it immediately on the dashboard.
  • Win/loss reason — so after six months you know exactly why you are winning and losing

Most home service owners write fifteen estimates a month and follow up on zero. They assume silence means no. In reality, forty percent of homeowners who did not book immediately were still shopping — and the first contractor who calls back wins. The estimate tracker makes the follow-up automatic. At an eight hundred dollar average job, converting two more estimates per month pays for every tool you own.

4. Invoice Generator — From Job Complete to Bill Sent in 30 Seconds

When a job is marked Done in the Scheduler, the Invoice Generator auto-populates:

  • Customer name, address, date of service
  • Job description (pulled from Scheduler)
  • Hours worked, labor rate, materials used, materials cost
  • Subtotal, tax, total
  • Status: Draft, Sent, Viewed (if using an online invoice link), Paid, Overdue
  • Print-ready format — copy into your invoice template and send

No more re-typing the same customer information from the whiteboard into QuickBooks at 9 PM on a Saturday. The invoice is ninety percent done before you open it.

5. Payment Tracker — Accounts Receivable Without QuickBooks

Every invoice that is Sent but not Paid lives here:

  • Customer, invoice number, amount, date sent, due date
  • Aging buckets: Current (0 to 30 days), 31 to 60 days, 61 to 90 days, 90-plus days
  • Auto-calculated DSO (days sales outstanding) — the average number of days between finishing work and getting paid
  • Auto-flag: Any customer past 30 days turns amber. Past 60 days turns red. The owner sees it on the dashboard and makes the call.
  • Payment method (check, credit card, ACH, financing) — so you know which customers always pay late and can require payment upfront next time

A 3-tech shop with sixty thousand dollars in AR at any given time, with an average DSO of forty two days, has roughly twenty eight thousand dollars in cash that should be in the bank but is sitting in customers' inboxes. The payment tracker moves that number down.

6. Weekly Admin Dashboard — The One-Page Business Snapshot

Every Monday morning, open one tab and see:

This Week's Dashboard

  • Hours worked vs hours billed: Are your techs billable at least seventy percent of their clocked time? If not, where is the time going?
  • Estimates given vs estimates won: What is your close rate this week? This month? Is it trending up or down?
  • Invoices sent vs invoices paid: How much did you bill this week and how much actually hit the bank?
  • Revenue collected: Actual cash in — the only number that matters.
  • Outstanding AR: Who still owes you money, sorted by amount (largest first) so you make the most valuable calls first.
  • Estimates not followed up: The red list — these are dollars you are about to lose if you do not call today.

The dashboard takes twenty minutes to update each week. It replaces four hours of mental math, sticky-note arithmetic, and the sinking feeling on Sunday night that you forgot something important.
