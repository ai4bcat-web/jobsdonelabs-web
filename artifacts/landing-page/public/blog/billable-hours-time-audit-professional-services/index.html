The 5-day time audit: find out where your money really goes

You know you're losing billable hours. You just don't know where. The 5-day time audit answers that question with hard data — not gut feelings.

Download the free time-audit spreadsheet (Google Sheets). For 5 consecutive working days, track every 15-minute block into one of five buckets:

The 5 audit categories

  1. Billable-Client-Work — the actual legal/accounting/consulting work you bill for
  2. Billable-Client-Communication — emails, calls, and meetings with clients about active matters
  3. Non-Billable-Admin — email sorting, filing, scheduling, invoicing, formatting, data entry, chasing signatures
  4. Non-Billable-Business-Development — proposals, networking, content, referral relationships
  5. Pure-Waste — social media, context-switching recovery, looking for that file you know you saved somewhere
25–35%

That's how much of the average solo professional's workday lands in buckets 3 and 5 — non-billable admin and pure waste. At $350/hour × 1.5 hours/day × 220 working days = $115,500/year in lost billable capacity. Even at $200/hour, that's $66,000/year.

The spreadsheet auto-calculates your leakage. At the end of the 5 days, you'll have a single number: this is exactly how much money you're leaving on the table every year. That number is the motivation you need for what comes next.

The 3-bucket system: Automate, Delegate, Eliminate

Once you know which tasks are draining your time, sort every non-billable activity into one of three buckets:

BucketWhat goes hereExample
AutomateRepetitive, rules-based tasks a computer can doEmail sorting by client, invoice generation, calendar scheduling, doc templates
DelegateTasks requiring judgment but not your expertise — a $15–25/hr virtual assistant can handle themFollow-up scheduling, data entry, file organization, travel booking, client intake forms
EliminateTasks you do out of habit that add zero client value — stop doing them entirelyRe-reading emails 3 times, manually formatting the same header on every doc, double-checking things that were already right

The rule of thumb: if a task doesn't require your license, your signature, or your brain — it belongs in Automate or Delegate. If it adds no value to anyone, eliminate it cold.

The top 5 admin time sinks and exactly how to automate each

These five tasks consume nearly every minute in the "Non-Billable-Admin" bucket. Here's the step-by-step fix for each — all starting at $0.

1. Email sorting and triaging (20 min/day recovered)

Set up Gmail filters that auto-label every incoming email by client, matter, or project. Create labels: "Client-Acme," "Client-Baker," "Prospect," "Newsletter," "Admin." Then add auto-archive rules for CC-only emails and newsletters you never read. When you open your inbox, client emails are already sorted — you see Acme matters together, Baker matters together. No more scrolling through 40 emails to find the thread.

Setup time: 30 minutes. Cost: $0 (included with Gmail/Google Workspace). Recovered: 15–20 min/day.

2. Calendar scheduling (15 min/day recovered)

Use Calendly free tier. Set up one event type: "30-Minute Call." Connect it to your Google Calendar so it only shows availability during your open slots. Replace every "what time works for you?" email with a single line: "You can grab a time on my calendar here: [Calendly link]." That's 5 back-and-forth emails eliminated per meeting booked.

Setup time: 15 minutes. Cost: $0 (Calendly free tier). Recovered: 10–15 min/day.

3. Document formatting and template population (10 min/day recovered)

Stop rewriting engagement letters, retention agreements, and follow-up emails from scratch. Build one master template for each document type with merge fields for client name, date, scope, and fee. Store them in a Google Drive folder called "Templates." When you need a document, open the template, fill in 4 fields, send. This is 90 seconds instead of 12 minutes of retyping the same boilerplate.

Setup time: 2 hours (one-time). Cost: $0 (Google Docs). Recovered: 8–12 min/day.

4. Timesheet tracking and invoice generation (15 min/day recovered)

Use Toggl Track free tier. Start a timer when you begin working on a client matter, stop it when you switch. At month-end, export the time log into a pre-built invoice template (Google Sheets) that auto-populates: client name, hours worked, rate, total. The invoice populates from the timesheet data in under 60 seconds.

Setup time: 45 minutes. Cost: $0 (Toggl free tier + Google Sheets). Recovered: 12–15 min/day.

5. Automated invoice delivery (5 min/day recovered)

Use Gmail's scheduled send or a free tool like Mailmeteor to batch-deliver invoices to clients on a set schedule. Create a recurring calendar reminder: "Send invoices — 1st of month." The invoices are already built from #4 above; sending them takes 30 seconds per client instead of 5 minutes of composing individual emails.

Setup time: 10 minutes. Cost: $0. Recovered: 5–8 min/day.

Total recovered across all 5 automations: 60–75 minutes of billable time per day. At $350/hour × 1.25 hours × 220 days = $96,250/year in recovered capacity. Total setup cost across all 5: $0.

When to hire vs. when to automate

Not everything should be automated. Some tasks need a human — just not you. Here's the decision framework:

Hire a VA ($15–25/hr) when…Automate with tools ($0–50/mo) when…
The task requires judgment — "which of these 5 emails is actually urgent?"The task follows rules — "if email is from Client X, label it Client-X and skip inbox"
It involves talking to humans — client intake calls, scheduling conflicts, vendor follow-upsIt involves data — copying from one system to another, formatting, calculating, sorting
It happens irregularly or varies each time — "research this specific thing"It happens the same way every time — "every month, export timesheet, populate invoice"
You have 20+ hours/week to offload and the math worksYou have 5–10 hours/week to recover and don't want to manage a person

The smart sequence: automate first, then delegate the remainder. Automating the repetitive stuff shrinks the admin pile. What's left is the human-judgment work — perfect for a part-time VA at $15–25/hour. A VA handling 10 hours/week costs $600–$1,000/month. If that 10 hours is now billable at $350/hour, that's $14,000/month in new capacity for $1,000/month in cost. A 14x return.

The template library: stop rewriting the same documents

Most professionals have 5–8 document types they create over and over: engagement letters, proposals, follow-up emails, status updates, final reports. Each one takes 15–45 minutes from scratch — but 80% of the content is identical every time.

Build a template library in Google Drive:

  • Engagement letter — merge fields: client name, scope paragraph, fee structure, start date
  • Proposal — merge fields: client name, problem statement, solution overview, timeline, investment
  • Project status update — merge fields: client name, period, accomplishments, next steps, blockers
  • Invoice cover email — merge fields: client name, period, total, due date, payment link
  • Follow-up after proposal — merge fields: client name, proposal date, value-add link, call-to-action

Each template takes 2 hours to build once. Each use saves 20–30 minutes. If you use each template 15–20 times per year, the time saved is 25–50 hours — the equivalent of a full workweek you just got back.

Store the folder at drive.google.com > Templates > [Client-Work]. When you need a document, open the template, fill in the 4–6 merge fields, and send. Three minutes instead of thirty.

How do I automate admin tasks at a small law firm?

Start with the 5 biggest time sinks: (1) Set up Gmail filters + labels to auto-sort client emails by matter — saves 15–20 min/day. (2) Use Calendly free tier for scheduling — eliminates 5 back-and-forth emails per appointment. (3) Build document templates with merge fields for engagement letters and retention agreements. The rule of thumb: if the task doesn't require your specific expertise and someone can do it for less than a third of your billable rate, delegate it. Most solo consultants can offload 10–15 hours/week within the first month.
