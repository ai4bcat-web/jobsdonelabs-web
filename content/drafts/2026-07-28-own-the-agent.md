## Why Mid-Market Companies Are Quietly Replacing SaaS With AI Agents They Own

Last month I closed a revenue-share agreement with a partner. My Claude reviewed his draft. His Claude reviewed mine. Our two assistants went back and forth twice, the versions matched, and we both signed. I'm not sure he even opened the actual document.

Five years ago that was $8,000 in legal fees and three weeks of back-and-forth. This time it was about forty minutes.

I keep coming back to that moment, because it's a small version of a much bigger shift I'm watching play out across mid-market companies right now: **the done-for-you (DFY) model is quietly eating the SaaS model.** The tools that used to sell for $99/month are getting replaced by custom builds that live *inside* the company. And once you've felt the difference, it's hard to go back.

### Off-the-shelf software optimizes for the average company. A custom agent optimizes for *your* company.

Here's how I learned this the expensive way. I run a nine-truck asset-based freight brokerage in Chicago, and I could not find a TMS that fit how we actually operate. So I stopped looking and started building my own dashboard, connecting all the SaaS platforms we already paid for.

The thing I needed most wasn't in any of them: monitoring the real fuel pricing my drivers were specifically paying, day to day. Every out-of-the-box tool gave me a generic average. None of them gave me *my* numbers. Once I built it custom, I finally had the datapoints the software was never going to offer me — because that software was built for the average brokerage, not mine.

That's the whole shift in one sentence. **When you rent software, you conform to the tool. When you own an agent, the tool conforms to you.**

### This isn't a "someday" trend — it's already in how I run my life

It's not just the business. I had Claude write me a 90-day workout plan built around specific goals. I get my blood tested monthly and compare it against my baseline, and I built a tool that recommends changes to my diet and supplements based on those results. I've started asking a question that would've sounded ridiculous two years ago: *does anyone pay a human — or a subscription — for anything anymore?*

The honest answer, more and more, is: only where it truly requires a human. Everything that's really just "structured work that follows rules" is becoming an agent you own.

### What this means for mid-market operators

Companies would rather own the agent than rent the tool — and they're right to. A custom automation that lives inside your company:

- **Fits your process** instead of forcing you to fit it
- **Connects the tools you already pay for** instead of adding a sixth dashboard nobody logs into
- **Surfaces your data, your way** — the numbers off-the-shelf software will never expose
- **Is an asset you own**, not a rising monthly bill you rent forever

The $99/month tool optimizes for a million companies. The custom agent optimizes for one: yours.
