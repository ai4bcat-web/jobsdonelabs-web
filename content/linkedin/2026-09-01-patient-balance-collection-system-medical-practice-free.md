# Your Practice Writes Off 60% of Patient A/R. The Fix Isn't More Staff — It's a Free 3-Tier System.

Most medical practices write off 60% of patient balances over 90 days. Not because patients can't pay, but because nobody on staff wants to make the collection call. A free patient balance collection system with automated text reminders and an A/R aging dashboard can flip that number without a single awkward conversation.

I've seen this pattern in my own businesses. Not medical practices specifically, but the same structural problem: a monthly report with a terrifying number that slides into a folder, then a drawer, then gets handed to the accountant at year-end when half the money is unrecoverable. The difference in freight is that loads don't have feelings. Patients do. And the psychological barrier of "calling someone to ask for money" is a bigger blocker than any software gap.

## The Real Problem Isn't Collections. It's Avoidance.

Here's what the data says. A three-provider primary care practice typically carries $80,000 to $150,000 in patient balances past 90 days at any given time. Of that, roughly 60% ($48,000 to $90,000) will never be collected. Not because patients are deadbeats. Because nobody asked.

The reasons are painfully predictable. The front desk is checking in patients while the waiting room fills up. The billing manager is working denied claims with 30-day appeal windows. The office manager has fourteen other priorities. And the one person who finally makes the call reaches the patient at 5:30 PM while they're driving home. They promise to "look at the statement and call back." They never do.

This isn't a staffing problem. Adding another biller doesn't fix it because even the best biller has the same emotional aversion to collection calls that everyone else does. The practices that collect 80% or more of patient balances don't have more staff, they have a system that doesn't depend on someone finding the emotional energy to make uncomfortable phone calls all afternoon.

## The 3-Tier System That Replaces Phone Calls With Process

I published a [free patient balance collection spreadsheet](https://www.jobsdonelabs.ai/blog/patient-balance-collection-system-medical-practice-free/) that replaces the "call everyone" approach with escalating touchpoints. Most patients pay at Tier 1 or 2, without ever speaking to a human. Your staff only makes phone calls for the small subset of balances that reach Tier 3, and by then the patient has already received two reminders.

The tiers are simple. Tier 1 is a mailed statement with a payment link, sent the day a balance ages past 30 days. A handwritten "Questions? Call us" note on the statement improves response rates by 30% on its own. Tier 2 is a text reminder fourteen days later: friendly, non-confrontational, with a one-click payment link. Practices that add Tier 2 texts see payment rates jump from roughly 35% to 65% within thirty days. Tier 3 is one personal phone call at day 28, and only for balances over $200. By this point, most patients have already paid.

The phone script matters too. It should sound like a practice administrator helping someone resolve something they forgot about, not a collections agency. "I wanted to make sure you received the statement we mailed. Sometimes these get lost" opens the conversation without putting the patient on the defensive. The full script is in the spreadsheet along with email and text templates your front desk can use immediately.

The spreadsheet has four tabs. A patient balance ledger with every outstanding account sorted by days since last contact. An aging summary that auto-calculates totals by bucket. And a payment plan monitor for patients on monthly arrangements, flagging anyone who misses two consecutive payments before a $50-a-month plan becomes a $600 delinquent balance.

The whole thing is designed for a fifteen-minute Monday morning routine. Open the dashboard, sort by days since last contact, mail Tier 1 statements, send Tier 2 texts, note the two or three Tier 3 calls your office manager needs to make that week. Done. The folder stays empty and the ninety-day column stays small.
