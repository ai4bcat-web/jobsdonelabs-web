## The ABC Method Most Shops Skip

ABC classification sorts inventory by annual usage value: unit cost multiplied by how many you burn through in a year.

A items are your high-cost components, specialty materials, and long-lead-time parts. They represent roughly 70% of your total inventory value but only 10 to 20% of your SKUs. Count them monthly. When a $300 specialty fastener is off by 50 units, that's $15,000 in phantom inventory on one part number.

B items are mid-range. Counted quarterly. Another 20% of your inventory value.

C items are bulk fasteners, consumables, and commodities. They make up half your SKU count but only 10% of your inventory investment. When a box of M6 bolts is off by 50, you are out twelve dollars, not twelve thousand.

A typical 500-SKU shop running ABC cycle counts needs about 20 minutes a day. Not 20 hours. Not a production shutdown. Twenty minutes to prevent your next line stoppage.

## The Real Fix: Root-Cause Tracking

Here is the part most guides skip. Counting finds the problem. Root-cause tracking prevents it from happening again.

Every variance gets a category: receiving error, picking error, scrap not recorded, data entry error, supplier short-ship, or theft. A 20-person machine shop tracked their discrepancies for three months and found receiving errors caused 42% of all inventory variance dollars. The fix was a ten-minute verification step at the dock: count every line item against the packing slip before signing. Cost: zero dollars. Impact: inventory accuracy went from 91% to 96% in six weeks.

That shop had been doing annual physical counts for seven years and never knew receiving was the problem. They never categorized the discrepancies, so they never saw the pattern. After three months of disciplined root-cause tracking, the top two categories typically account for 75 to 85% of all discrepancy dollars. Fix those two processes and your accuracy jumps from 92% to 97%+ without changing anything else.
