## The Math That Should Terrify Every Home Service Owner

Here's the scenario. A five-person HVAC shop does forty-two installs in a single month. Forty of those customers are genuinely happy — the work was on time, on budget, and cleaner than when the tech arrived. But only two of them leave a Google review.

Meanwhile, a competitor with a 4.6-star average and eighty-seven reviews — most of them months or years old — shows up first in every local search result. They win the call. Not because they earned it. Because they have more people saying so publicly.

This isn't anecdotal. A local business with fewer than fifteen total Google reviews loses an estimated twenty-five to thirty-five percent of potential calls to competitors with higher review counts — regardless of star rating. Google's local algorithm weights recency and volume over average score. Your perfect 5.0 with twelve reviews loses to their 4.6 with eighty-seven, every single time. The 2026 data from Google Business Profile analytics across ten thousand businesses confirms reviews are the number one local ranking factor — ahead of proximity, ahead of keyword relevance, ahead of everything.

The problem isn't that your customers are unhappy. The problem is you don't have a system for asking. Your techs might mention it, or they might not. The office sends a follow-up email — sometimes. The owner feels awkward asking because it sounds like begging. And so forty happy customers go home, open their phones, and call the competitor with eighty-seven reviews.

## Three Natural Moments When Asking Doesn't Feel Like Asking

Timing is everything. Ask at the wrong moment and you sound like a marketer. Ask at the right moment and you sound like a professional finishing a job well done. Based on conversion data across hundreds of home service businesses, there are exactly three moments when a review request converts at the highest rate:

- **Job completion** — the tech hands over the invoice. Customer satisfaction peaks. A casual mention converts roughly 42% of the time.
- **Three-day follow-up email** — the customer has lived with the work. Everything is working. A quick email with a direct review link converts roughly 28%.
- **Thirty-day warranty check-in call** — you're not asking for a review. You're checking whether the work held up. But when the customer says "everything's great," the door opens. About 19% conversion rate.

Three touches per customer, forty jobs a month — that math generates roughly twenty-five reviews every month instead of two. That's the difference between losing calls and being the business everyone calls first.
