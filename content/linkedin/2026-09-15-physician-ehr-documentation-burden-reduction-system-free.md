# EHR Documentation Burns 2 Hours for Every Patient Hour. The Fix Costs $0.

Physicians spend 2 hours on EHR documentation for every 1 hour of direct patient care, roughly 960 hours a year. At a loaded rate of $125 per hour, that is $120,000 per physician spent on data entry instead of patients.

That number is not a rounding error. It is from the AMA. It holds across all specialties, all EHR platforms, and all practice sizes. For a 4-physician practice, the annual cost approaches half a million dollars in physician labor that produces zero billable visits.

## The AI Scribe Pitch Is Everywhere. It Is Also a Distraction.

Every vendor in healthcare tech is selling the same solution right now: ambient AI scribes. Install our software. Let the AI listen. Never type a note again.

Here is what they do not tell you. Ambient scribes cost $100 to $400 per physician per month. They require IT approval, data-sharing agreements, and weeks of implementation. They also require you to trust a third-party AI with every word spoken in your exam rooms. For many small practices, that is three dealbreakers before the demo even ends.

And the worst part: ambient AI scribes do not fix the underlying problem. They paper over it. Your EHR still has too many clicks. Your templates still do not match your workflow. Your MA still writes vitals on a paper superbill that you re-enter yourself. The AI scribe just makes it slightly less painful to do all the same broken things.

## The Five Non-Software Fixes That Work With Any EHR

I spent the last month researching how small practices actually reduce documentation time. Not the vendor case studies. Not the conference keynotes. The real practices: two to eight physicians, independent or loosely affiliated, no IT department, no budget for Epic optimization consultants.

The ones getting results are not buying new tools. They are changing workflow. Five specific changes, each independently measurable, each costing zero dollars.

**1. Smart phrases.** Build 20 text expanders for your 20 most common visit types. A primary care practice covers 80% of encounters with 20 visit types. Type ".wellvisit" and the entire note populates: normal exam, anticipatory guidance, CPT codes. Three to 8 minutes saved per encounter. Across 20 encounters a day, that is 12 to 32 hours per month reclaimed.

**2. Document in the room.** Type the HPI and assessment while the patient is present. "Let me summarize what we discussed." The patient hears you being thorough. Your future self skips the memory-reconstruction tax that adds 30 to 40 percent to notes written more than 4 hours later.

**3. Let your MA enter the data they already collect.** Vitals, medications, allergies, ROS. Your MA is already gathering this during rooming. Have them type it directly into the note fields instead of writing it on a paper superbill. Three to 5 minutes saved per encounter.

**4. Dictate the assessment and plan.** Most physicians speak three times faster than they type. Use your EHR's built-in voice-to-text with a structured template. A follow-up note goes from 12 minutes of typing to 4 minutes of dictation plus 2 minutes of review.

**5. Close every note within 24 hours.** Notes written more than 48 hours after the encounter take 50 to 70 percent longer. The brain forgets. The cognitive load compounds. Close the note the same day. After two weeks, the habit forms and the mental backlog disappears.
