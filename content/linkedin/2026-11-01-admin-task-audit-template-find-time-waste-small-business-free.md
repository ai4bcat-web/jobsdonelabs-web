## The Three-Step Framework

Here's how it works:

**Step one: The week-in-the-life audit log.** For five working days, you log every administrative task as it happens. Not at the end of the day from memory. In the moment. Six data points per task: what you did, when, how long it took, frequency pattern, category, and system used. After five days, you have a complete picture. Most owners are stunned by the concentration. Two or three task categories dominate 60 to 80 percent of the total admin hours. That concentration is good news. You don't need to fix everything. You need to fix the big three.

**Step two: The automatable-ness scorecard.** Each task gets scored 0 to 4 on four questions: Does it have a clear trigger? Is the action predictable? Does the data already live in a system? Can you fix it with tools you already have? Tasks scoring 3 or 4 are your automation targets. Tasks scoring 0 or 1 stay with a person.

**Step three: The 2×2 priority matrix.** Plot every task by time-suck (minutes per week) versus automation score. Quadrant 1 (high time-suck, easy to automate) is where you start. These are the tasks that deliver 10 to 20 times the impact of smaller fixes. Invoice reminders. Document follow-up sequences. Appointment confirmations. Fix one Quadrant 1 task and you recover more hours than fixing five Quadrant 3 tasks.
